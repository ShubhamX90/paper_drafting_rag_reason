\subsection{Refusal recall improves substantially}
\label{sec:refusal}

The largest and cleanest effect is the answer/refuse decision. Refusal F1
separates completely: every baseline configuration scores $\leq0.429$ and every
trace-tuned configuration $\geq0.826$, with \emph{no overlap}, a mean improvement
of $+0.709$. This is not over-caution. Decision accuracy also rises in every cell
and crosses the answer-everything floor: the benchmark is $82.6\%$ answerable, so
answering everything scores $0.826$ for free, and no baseline configuration
exceeds that floor by more than the $\pm0.034$ noise interval while every
trace-tuned cell reaches $0.927$--$0.976$. Behaviour Adherence improves alongside
the decision but unevenly, from $+0.096$ for Mistral-7B to $+0.000$ for
Qwen2.5-7B, an ordering that tracks baseline instruction-following.
Appendix~\ref{app:full} gives the per-cell recall and accuracy figures and the
Llama-3.1-8B case that shows why the two decision numbers must be read together.

\subsection{Learnt behaviour is prompt-invariant}
\label{sec:internalization}

Removing the prompt scaffolding is close to a null operation for fine-tuned
models and close to catastrophic for baseline ones. The minimal
condition is a genuinely sparse instruction, forty words that never name the
trace, the taxonomy or the citation format, so a model that still produces them
is reproducing a learned mapping rather than following the prompt. Component by
component, no trace-tuned model varies by more than the $\pm0.034$ noise floor
across the ladder, against baseline spreads of $0.240$--$0.398$ on refusal F1 and
$0.620$--$0.823$ on factual grounding; under the minimal prompt baseline
grounding falls to $0.031$--$0.108$ while trace-tuned models sustain
$0.792$--$0.857$ (Table~\ref{tab:internalization}). The result also reproduces on
citation measurements built after and independently of CATS.
Figure~\ref{fig:internal} plots the full ladder, and
Appendix~\ref{app:figs} gives the runtime-condition and citation-sufficiency
figures.

\subsection{What trace supervision does \emph{not} buy}
\label{sec:whatdoesnot}

Two components show no consistent gain. Single-truth recall is positive in only
$7$ of $12$ cells (sign test $p=0.77$), a well-powered null: fine-tuned models
are not recovering target answers more often. Factual grounding is not directly
comparable across run types, because it is averaged over
$\textit{fg\_n}=608+\textit{missed refusals}$, so a baseline model that rarely
refuses is additionally scored on ${\sim}130$ items whose evidence is
insufficient by construction; bounding FG on the like-for-like population, the
intervals overlap in $6$ of $12$ cells, every one of them a strict or runtime
cell (Table~\ref{tab:fgdenom}; see Appendix~\ref{app:deconfound}). Trace supervision
therefore raises grounding when the instruction is sparse, but under a
well-specified prompt no consistent advantage is established. Given a
well-specified prompt a baseline model already produces competitive grounded
answers; what it lacks is the judgment to decide whether to answer at all, and
the ability to retain any of this without being told the procedure. That is what
trace supervision supplies, which is why these negative results are predictions
of our account rather than exceptions to it.
Appendix~\ref{app:deconfound} gives the per-cell bounds and the
instruction-volume effect for baseline models.

\subsection{Mechanism-matched oracle ablations}
\label{sec:oracle}

This ablation asks what the final response gains when the model is handed the
gold conflict type, the gold per-document notes, or both, instead of having to
infer them. Each condition replaces one stage of the trace with ground truth
and leaves the rest of the pipeline unchanged, so the resulting change isolates
what that stage contributes. They are diagnostic interventions, never
deployment settings.

Read as a single aggregate, their effect is small and the analysis stops there.
Read \emph{per metric}, with metrics the intervention should not affect acting
as a specificity control, the result is considerably stronger. Table~\ref{tab:oracle} and
Figure~\ref{fig:oraclefig} give the per-metric effect of each oracle, on which
the paragraphs below draw; Appendix~\ref{app:oracle} discusses them.

\paragraph{The conflict label moves behaviour and only behaviour.} Supplying the
gold Stage-2 label improves Behaviour Adherence ($+0.013$, $8/9$ cells,
$p=0.039$) and moves no other metric significantly; grounding, citation and
decision metrics are flat to slightly negative.

\paragraph{The document notes move grounding and only grounding.} Supplying gold
Stage-1 notes improves Factual Grounding ($+0.047$), answer quality ($+0.044$),
strict citation quality ($+0.041$), citation precision ($+0.033$) and citation
sufficiency ($+0.028$), each positive in \emph{every} cell. Behaviour shows a
small spillover, expected since correct document verdicts also constrain the
conflict inference and the answer/refuse decision is untouched, as it should be
since GR is upstream of both interventions.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} That each intervention moves the metrics its stage
governs and no others, and that the effects add ($|\Delta_{\text{both}}-
(\Delta_{\text{conflict}}+\Delta_{\text{notes}})|\leq0.012$), is a
construct-validity argument for the decomposition. For \emph{baseline} models
every oracle instead \emph{hurts}, catastrophically so for grounding under the
conflict label ($-0.238$), a second internalization result.
Appendix~\ref{app:oracle} gives the per-metric residuals, the behaviour
exception, the caveat on where the remaining error lives, and the baseline
breakdown.

\subsection{External prompting comparators}
\label{sec:comparators}

We compare against two prompting-only strategies applied to baseline models:
few-shot Chain-of-Thought \citep{wei2022cot} and Chain-of-Note-style retrieval
notes \citep{yu2024chainofnote}. Neither is fine-tuned; both receive the same
benchmark and evaluator (Table~\ref{tab:comparators}).

Both reach usable answer content, Chain-of-Note reaching factual grounding of
$0.52$--$0.67$, but neither acquires the answer/refuse decision. Chain-of-Note
\emph{never abstains}: zero correct refusals out of 128 on all three models,
landing exactly on the answer-everything floor. Few-shot Chain-of-Thought
abstains inconsistently rather than rarely, from $32/128$ to $85/128$ across
models. Structured prompting can therefore elicit conflict-aware \emph{answers}
without supplying conflict-aware \emph{judgment}, the distinction
\S\ref{sec:intro} draws. Appendix~\ref{app:citation} gives the per-model
abstention spread, why the Mistral-7B exception is a refusal prior rather than
acquired judgment, and Chain-of-Note's citation hygiene, the poorest of any
method examined.

\paragraph{Citation behaviour.} Trace supervision changes citation \emph{sufficiency}, not citation precision: precision is near-ceiling for every model regardless of tuning, while the recall side moves sharply. Appendix~\ref{app:citation} gives the seven-metric analysis, the definitions and the per-cell values.

\section{Validating the Evaluator}
\label{sec:human}

A judged metric is only as trustworthy as its judge, so we validated the CATS
committee against an independent human study: four reviewers double-judged 300
responses spanning both model families, both training conditions and all five
conflict types. The resulting reliability ordering is identical on two
independent instruments and bounds what we may claim. \textbf{Factual Grounding
and Single-Truth Recall are reliable} ($\kappa=0.78$ and $0.79$ against humans);
\textbf{Behaviour Adherence is not} ($\kappa=0.41$), with the weakness
concentrated in Complementary information and Misinformation, where human
reviewers also disagree most. We therefore report conflict-conditioned behaviour
qualitatively and do not interpret individual BA margins. Appendices
\ref{app:human} and~\ref{app:allagreement} give the study design, every agreement
statistic and the coverage caveats; any reading of a BA number should be checked
against them.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Trace supervision transforms the answer/refuse decision
and makes conflict-aware behaviour survive removal of the instruction describing
it; it does \emph{not} improve answer writing, because that deficit was never
there to fix; and the three stages behave as separable constructs, so the trace
is a measurement instrument rather than a formatting convention.

Three questions remain open. The residual error after both oracles is small but unattributed, and separating
Stage-3 synthesis failure from annotation noise and judge strictness needs a
targeted audit. Behaviour Adherence is the weakest instrument we report, so
conflict-behaviour claims should stay qualitative until it improves. And
misinformation remains the smallest class in the benchmark; expanding it without
perturbation-based synthesis is an open data-collection problem.

The practical consequence is a separation of concerns. A retrieval system that
answers well but cannot decide \emph{when} to answer is not a weaker version of
one that can; the two differ in a capability that no prompt in our ladder
supplied, and that supervising the reasoning did. Building such a system means
supervising the judgment, and evaluating it means scoring that judgment apart
from the answer it produces.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Per-document verdicts are never human-validated.} The Stage-1
verdicts (\textit{supports}, \textit{partially supports}, \textit{irrelevant})
that gate Factual Grounding eligibility are produced entirely by the annotation
committee, including frontier-model annotators, and are checked by human
reviewers at no stage. This is a different status from the conflict-type label,
which is reviewed twice, and it means the eligibility set on which every
grounding and citation number depends rests on model agreement rather than
human adjudication. The refusal-track validity check
(Appendix~\ref{app:refusalquality}) is indirect evidence that the verdicts
track something real, not a substitute for direct validation.

\paragraph{Evidence-bounded evaluation.} CATS scores whether an answer is
justified by the retrieved set, not whether it is true. A globally correct answer
unsupported by $D$ scores poorly. This is deliberate for retrieval-faithfulness
research and inappropriate if the question is unrestricted factual competence.
Within that scope, grounding is measured permissively: the judge is given seven
routes to support and instructed to bias toward inclusion
(Appendix~\ref{app:catsprompts}), so our grounding numbers are an upper bound
relative to a strict entailment criterion.

\paragraph{Behaviour Adherence reliability.} As \S\ref{sec:human} reports, BA is
the least reliable component ($\kappa=0.41$ against humans, $\alpha=0.46$ among
judges) and all observed BA differences fall inside that disagreement envelope.
BA-based conclusions here are qualitative. Relatedly, the judge committee uses a
priority-weighted rule under which the highest-priority judge cannot be
overruled by the other two on behaviour judgments once its self-reported
confidence exceeds $5/6$; behaviour is therefore closer to a single-judge
measurement than the panel size suggests, precisely in the disputed cases.

\paragraph{Shared model families between annotation and evaluation.} The
annotation committee and the evaluation committee draw on the same three
open-weight model families, with different weightings. The constructs differ
(document adjudication versus answer judging) and Grounded Refusal is
deterministic and outside the committee entirely, but the evaluator is partly
scoring labels produced by related models. The human study in \S\ref{sec:human}
is our external check on this and it covers two of the four model families.

\paragraph{Aggregate properties.} The type-balanced aggregate assigns $40\%$ of
its weight to refusal-required examples, which score decision correctness only,
against $17.4\%$ of the data; one type-3 refusal example carries roughly twelve
times the influence of one type-1 answerable example; and the misinformation
class, $5\%$ of the data, determines $20\%$ of the score. A model with zero
usable answer content but perfect refusal calibration would score $0.400$. This
is why we report components first and aggregates second
(Appendix~\ref{app:aggregate}).

\paragraph{Recipe asymmetry and scale.} Qwen models use recipe~K and
Llama/Mistral recipe~L; no completed Qwen matrix exists for recipe~L. Recipes
J/K/L change several ingredients together and are principled recipe revisions
rather than single-factor ablations. All models are in the 7B--32B range and
Mistral-7B remains over-abstaining under every recipe we tried, a model-family
limitation we report rather than omit.

\paragraph{Annotation quality.} Supporting quotes are snippet-\emph{anchored}
rather than fully verbatim: $81$--$83\%$ are exact contiguous substrings and
${\sim}96\%$ are traceable to the snippet, with ${\sim}4\%$ showing no detectable
overlap. Source-quality labels come from an explicit URL whitelist rule
(Appendix~\ref{app:annotation}), not from an independent credibility assessment,
and are systematically pushed toward \textit{low} on the refusal track, whose
upstream source uses placeholder URLs. Three benchmark
records carry a numeric conflict identifier inconsistent with their
authoritative textual label; we verified this did not affect any reported result.

\paragraph{Human review coverage.} The benchmark second review pass is
sequential rather than blind, so its agreement figures measure verification
consistency. Of the 943 consolidated training rows, 285 are consensus-completed
rather than genuine two-reviewer pairs and 105 of the 608 answerable benchmark
records appear only as consensus-completed entries. Several funnel stages
($1{,}878$ assigned candidates against $1{,}221$ reviewed; 200 curated refusals
against 128 released) have no retained per-record selector; we report the
counts as observed rather than inferring a rule.

\paragraph{Comparator scope.} The prompting comparators cover three of the four
model families and one prompt configuration each; they are not exhaustive
prompt-engineering baselines. One comparator metric shifted substantially
between our earlier evaluation and this one in a way most consistent with a
corrected answer-extraction step, so we base the comparator conclusion on
abstention behaviour and citation hygiene, which are stable across both, rather
than on grounding alone.

\section*{Ethical considerations}

\paragraph{Data provenance and licensing.} The released resource is derived from
publicly available research datasets and from live web retrieval. Source
provenance is preserved in every record and the dataset cards identify the
upstream collection for each source family so that downstream users can consult
the relevant licences before redistributing source-derived material. Retrieved
snippets are stored with their source URL and, where available, a timestamp;
because live retrieval is time-varying, the release should be treated as a
snapshot rather than a reproducible query against a current index.

\paragraph{Human annotators.} Human review was carried out by members of the
research group rather than by crowdworkers. Reviewer identifiers are retained in
the review artifacts for auditability but carry no demographic information and
we make no claims about recruitment, compensation or demographics that our
records do not support.

\paragraph{Intended use and risks.} The resource is intended for research on
evidence-grounded generation, abstention calibration and conflict-aware
evaluation. Two cautions apply. Conflict labels and answerability judgments
describe the \emph{supplied} evidence, not world truth and must not be read as
factual adjudications of the underlying questions, several of which concern
contested scientific or medical topics. Second, the source-quality field is a
coarse URL-whitelist heuristic retained as annotation metadata; it is not a
credibility ranking of publishers and should not be used as one.

\paragraph{Disclosure of AI assistance.} Frontier language models were used to
generate the synthetic annotations in our dataset and to evaluate model
performance as LLM-as-a-judge; the methodology for both is specified in
\S\ref{sec:data} and \S\ref{sec:cats}. They were also used for grammatical
refinement of the text. All experimental design, analysis and claims are the
authors' own and the paper is an accurate representation of the underlying
work.
