\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation is unreliable when retrieved documents disagree,
go stale, or fail to support any answer. We attribute this to a deficit in
\emph{judgment} rather than in answer generation, and separate the two
empirically. We release a conflict-aware RAG benchmark of $1{,}679$ examples and
$10{,}343$ retrieved documents, each annotated with a relevance judgment, an
extracted supporting fact, a source-quality assessment, and complete annotator
vote tallies. We supervise open-weight models to generate evidence traces
comprising per-document adjudication, cross-document conflict analysis, and
conflict-conditioned response synthesis, and evaluate them with CATS, which
scores grounded refusal, behavioural adherence, factual grounding, and
single-truth recall as separate components, each validated against an
independent human study. Trace supervision transforms the refusal decision:
refusal F1 rises from at most $0.429$ to at least $0.826$ across every model and
prompt setting, and no baseline configuration exceeds the answer-everything
baseline. Behaviour also becomes prompt-invariant: across a strict-to-minimal
instruction ladder no trace-tuned model moves by more than the $\pm0.034$
measurement noise on any component, against baseline spreads of up to $0.823$;
grounding under the sparsest instruction is $0.792$--$0.857$ tuned against
$0.031$--$0.108$ baseline. Answer
quality does not improve: single-truth recall is unchanged ($p=0.77$), and the
grounding advantage under well-specified prompts does not survive correction of
two measurement artefacts we identify. Oracle ablations show that gold
annotations for one stage move only the metrics that stage governs, and that the
effects add, indicating the three stages are separable constructs. The human
study finds behavioural adherence substantially less reliable than the other
three components.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-augmented generation conditions a generator on a small set of retrieved
snippets, ideally with faithful attribution
\citep{lewis2020rag,borgeaud2022,gao2024retrievalaugmentedgenerationlargelanguage,guu2020realm}.
In practice, retrieval rarely returns a coherent evidence set, instead
producing documents that vary in relevance, quality, credibility, and recency.
The retrieved documents may contain conflicting claims, provide only partial
support, or collectively be insufficient to answer the user's query.

Such a set demands a sequence of judgments before a single word of the answer is
written: which document says what, how the documents relate to one another and
whether what remains is sufficient. A system that treats the snippets as
interchangeable context produces a fluent, confidently wrong answer; a system
that refuses whenever sources disagree is no better, since disagreement is often
compatible with a properly scoped answer.

Existing work addresses parts of this, but each stops short of a different part.
\textit{DRAGged into Conflicts} \citep{cattan2025dragged} supervises the conflict
label rather than the reasoning that produces it; \textit{Chain-of-Note}
\citep{yu2024chainofnote} produces per-document notes without characterising how the
retrieved set coheres; and \textit{Trust-Score}/\textit{Trust-Align}
\citep{song2025trustalign} score refusal and citation behaviour without conditioning
the expected response on the kind of conflict present. Closest in framing, \citet{wu2026conflictaware} also fine-tune open models on
conflict signals, but derive those signals from generation probabilities rather
than from an explicit textual record, \citet{wang2025conflictingevidence}
resolve conflicting evidence by inference-time multi-agent debate rather than
supervision, and \citet{wang2026conflictrag} pair a learned conflict detector
with principled credibility scoring, where our source-quality field is a coarse
URL heuristic. \citet{muhamed2025refusalbench} evaluate selective refusal
under flawed context and independently find it to be a trainable capability,
which is the deficit our results localise. The gap that remains is a single
framework in which per-document reasoning, cross-document conflict inference and
justified abstention are supervised together and evaluated together.

We close that gap with a \emph{structured evidence trace}. Before answering, the
model exposes a three-stage record of its own adjudication
(Figure~\ref{fig:rag}): \textbf{Stage~1} assigns each snippet a verdict with an
extracted key fact and a source-quality label; \textbf{Stage~2} aggregates those
judgments into one of five conflict patterns, following the taxonomy of
\citet{cattan2025dragged}; \textbf{Stage~3} produces a citation-linked answer or
a justified abstention, conditioned on that pattern (\S\ref{sec:method}). The
trace is emitted under a fixed output contract rather than as free-form
reasoning, so it is machine-checkable and a failure is attributable to a stage
rather than to the system as a whole. \citet{liu2026craft} share this philosophy,
training auditable traces by reinforcement learning on multi-hop QA rather than
supervising conflict adjudication. Prompting can produce conflict-aware answers; it cannot produce
conflict-aware judgment, and separating the two empirically is this paper's
central test.

\paragraph{A benchmark that can carry the claim.} Testing this requires evaluation data the training distribution did not shape, so
we built an independent 736-example benchmark with \emph{zero} identifier overlap
with training, verified on the released files rather than asserted. Because
open-web retrieval cannot surface some conflict regimes, a curated track supplies
them and its composition is reported per class (\S\ref{sec:data}).

\paragraph{Contributions.} \textbf{(1) A conflict-aware RAG resource}
(\S\ref{sec:data}): 943 training/validation examples and a disjoint 736-example
benchmark, with $10{,}343$ adjudicated evidence units carrying a verdict, key
fact, snippet-anchored quote, rationale, source-quality label and the full
annotator vote tally. \textbf{(2) Evidence that trace supervision buys judgment,
not answer quality} (\S\ref{sec:results}): refusal calibration and
prompt-invariance improve; single-truth recall and grounding under
well-specified prompts do not. \textbf{(3) CATS} (\S\ref{sec:cats}): an
evaluation framework scoring a conflict-aware response as four components rather
than one number, each reported over its own applicability denominator and
separately validated against an independent human study. \textbf{(4) Oracle
ablations} (\S\ref{sec:oracle}): supplying gold information one stage at a time
localises which stage limits the system and gives construct-validity evidence for
the three-stage decomposition.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Knowledge and Evidence Conflicts}

We scope this paper to conflicts \emph{among retrieved documents}. Conflict
between a model's parametric knowledge and retrieved evidence, the
context--memory setting targeted by much recent work
\citep{huo2025microact,zhang2025faithfulrag,ye2026seeingthrough}, is out of
scope: our systems are evaluated only on whether they reason correctly over the
supplied set.

Retrieval-augmented generation conditions a model on documents drawn from a large
corpus at generation time
\citep{guu2020realm,lewis2020rag,izacard2023atlas,borgeaud2022,gao2024retrievalaugmentedgenerationlargelanguage,ram-etal-2023-context}.
Retrieved documents routinely carry conflicting information, which complicates
generation and has become a research area of its own
\citep{xu2024knowledgeconflictsllmssurvey}. \textit{ConflictBank} \citep{su2024conflictbankbenchmarkevaluatinginfluence} evaluates how models handle conflicts within retrieved knowledge, within parametric knowledge and in their interplay, pairing Wikidata claims with naturally occurring or semantically constructed contradictory evidence. \textit{WikiContradict} \citep{hou2024wikicontradict} benchmarks factual and temporal robustness on real-world contradictions between Wikipedia passages, with human-annotated instances. \textit{ECON} \citep{jiayang2024econ} studies detection and resolution of evidence conflicts directly.
\textit{DRAGged into Conflicts} \citep{cattan2025dragged} is closest to our
setting: it proposes a conflict taxonomy spanning temporal, debatable,
misinformation and complementary cases and demonstrates that models often fail
to adapt their responses without explicit conflict-aware guidance. Most of this
literature is concerned with \emph{detecting} or \emph{classifying} conflict.
Our framework instead supervises the reasoning that produces the
classification and, because that reasoning is emitted in a checkable format,
evaluates whether a model's document-level judgments, its conflict inference and
its final response behaviour are mutually consistent, rather than scoring only
the final answer.

\subsection{Reasoning Supervision, Multi-Hop and Robustness in RAG}

Several lines of work supervise or elicit intermediate reasoning.
\textit{Chain-of-Thought} prompting established that generating intermediate
steps improves complex reasoning \citep{wei2022cot}. \textit{Chain-of-Note}
\citep{yu2024chainofnote} adapts this to retrieval by generating sequential
reading notes for each retrieved document, improving robustness to noisy and
irrelevant passages and enabling a model to answer ``unknown'' when neither
retrieved nor intrinsic knowledge suffices. Adaptive adversarial training
\citep{fang2024enhancingnoiserobustnessretrievalaugmented} and robustness
training \citep{yoran2024makingretrievalaugmentedlanguagemodels} mitigate
retrieval noise and \textit{GSM-IC} \citep{shi2023largelanguagemodelseasily}
shows that irrelevant context substantially degrades reasoning.

Our work differs in two respects. First, each stage has a closed label set, so
Stage-1 and Stage-2 decisions are separately scorable against gold annotations
rather than merely inspectable. Second and more importantly, \textit{we treat
the reasoning trace as an object of evaluation rather than a means of improving
the answer.} The three-stage decomposition earns its place only if the stages
are genuinely separable, which we test directly by supplying gold information
for one stage at a time and asking whether each intervention moves the metrics
that stage should govern and no others (\S\ref{sec:oracle}).

\subsection{Measuring Trust, Groundedness and Conflict Alignment}

Trustworthiness metrics for RAG largely score an answer against the retrieved
set as a whole; what conflict-aware evaluation additionally requires is that the
\emph{expected} response depend on the kind of disagreement present, so that a
balanced summary and a decisive correction are not scored by the same rule.

Evaluations of RAG frequently assess end-to-end system quality
\citep{gao2024retrievalaugmentedgenerationlargelanguage,xu2024aliiceevaluatingpositionalfinegrained},
conflating retriever quality with generator capability
\citep{fan2024surveyragmeetingllms}. \textit{Trust-Score} \citep{song2025trustalign}
advances this by evaluating trustworthiness holistically through answer
correctness, citation reliability and refusal behaviour and its companion
\textit{Trust-Align} aligns models to improve that score; its held-out refusal
material forms the refusal track of our resource. \textit{FaithEval}
\citep{ming2025faitheval} probes whether models stay faithful to context under
adversarial or counterfactual conditions.

CATS extends this line by conditioning the expected response on the inferred
conflict type, evaluating grounding at claim level with citation sensitivity, and
reporting every component over its own applicability denominator, since the four
constructs apply to different subsets of examples and conflating them confounds
the comparison the framework is meant to support (\S\ref{sec:whatdoesnot}). We
validate the judges against an independent human study \citep{zheng2023judging}
and report which components that validation supports and which it does not
(\S\ref{sec:human}).
