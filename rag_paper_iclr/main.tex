\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets a language model answer a question by
first retrieving relevant documents and then writing an answer grounded in
them. In practice, however, retrieved documents often disagree with each
other, contain outdated information, or fail to support any answer at all.
Current systems have no reliable way to judge which of these documents to
trust before responding. We argue that this is a problem of judgment,
not of answer generation, and we test the two abilities separately. To do
this, we release a benchmark of $1{,}679$ examples covering $10{,}343$
retrieved, labeled documents, and we fine-tune open-weight (publicly released)
language models to produce a structured evidence trace before
answering. This trace checks each source individually, analyzes how the
sources conflict, and produces a final answer or a justified refusal. We
evaluate the results with a new framework we call CATS
(Conflict-Aware Trust Score), which scores four aspects of a response:
whether the model correctly declines to answer when no source supports one
(grounded refusal), how consistently it follows that decision across prompt
styles (behavioural adherence), whether the cited documents back its claims
(factual grounding), and whether it still finds the right answer when one
exists (single-truth recall). We validate the latter three against an
independent human study and compute grounded refusal deterministically. Trace
supervision substantially improves the refusal decision. Refusal F1
rises from as low as $0.429$ without it to at least $0.826$ with it, across
every model and prompt setting we test. Trace-tuned models are also far more
consistent across different prompt phrasings, while baseline behavior swings
widely. Answer quality itself does not improve: single-truth recall
is unchanged, and the grounding advantage we see under well-specified prompts
disappears once we correct two measurement artefacts we identify. Overall,
structured evidence traces make conflict-aware refusal reliable and
prompt-robust without improving the model's ability to find correct answers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-augmented generation conditions a generator on a small set of retrieved
snippets, ideally with faithful attribution
\citep{lewis2020rag,borgeaud2022,gao2024retrievalaugmentedgenerationlargelanguage,guu2020realm}.
In practice, retrieval rarely returns a coherent evidence set, instead
producing documents that vary in relevance, quality, credibility, and recency.
The retrieved documents may contain conflicting claims, provide only partial
support, or collectively be insufficient to answer the user's query.

Such a set demands a sequence of judgments before the model writes a single
word of its answer. These judgments are which document says what, how the
documents relate to one another, and whether what remains is sufficient. A
system that treats the snippets as interchangeable context produces a fluent,
confidently wrong answer. A system that refuses whenever sources disagree is
no better, since disagreement is often compatible with a properly scoped
answer.

Existing work addresses parts of this, but each stops short of a different part.
\textit{DRAGged into Conflicts} \citep{cattan2025dragged} supervises the conflict
label rather than the reasoning that produces it. \textit{Chain-of-Note}
\citep{yu2024chainofnote} produces per-document notes without characterising how the
retrieved set coheres. \textit{Trust-Score}/\textit{Trust-Align}
\citep{song2025trustalign} score refusal and citation behaviour without conditioning
the expected response on the kind of conflict present. Closest in framing, \citet{wu2026conflictaware} also fine-tune open models on
conflict signals, but derive those signals from generation probabilities rather
than from an explicit textual record. \citet{wang2025conflictingevidence}
resolve conflicting evidence by inference-time multi-agent debate rather than
supervision. \citet{wang2026conflictrag} pair a learned conflict detector
with principled credibility scoring, where our source-quality field is a coarse
URL heuristic. \citet{muhamed2025refusalbench} evaluate selective refusal
under flawed context and independently find it to be a trainable capability,
which is the deficit our results localise. The gap that remains is a single
framework that both supervises and evaluates per-document reasoning,
cross-document conflict inference, and justified abstention together.

We close that gap with a \emph{structured evidence trace}. Before answering, the
model exposes a three-stage record of its own adjudication
(Figure~\ref{fig:rag}). \textbf{Stage~1} addresses which document says what: it
assigns each snippet a verdict with an extracted key fact and a
source-quality label. \textbf{Stage~2} addresses how the documents relate to
one another: it aggregates those judgments into one of five conflict
patterns, following the taxonomy of \citet{cattan2025dragged}. \textbf{Stage~3}
addresses whether what remains is sufficient to answer: it produces a
citation-linked answer or a justified abstention, conditioned on that pattern
(\S\ref{sec:method}). The model emits the trace under a fixed output contract
rather than as free-form reasoning. This makes it machine-checkable, so a
failure is attributable to a stage rather than to the system as a whole.
\citet{liu2026craft} share this philosophy,
training auditable traces by reinforcement learning on multi-hop QA rather than
supervising conflict adjudication. Prompting can produce conflict-aware answers, but it cannot produce
conflict-aware judgment. Separating the two empirically is this paper's
central test.

\textbf{A benchmark that can carry the claim.} Testing this requires evaluation
data that the training distribution did not shape. We built an independent
736-example benchmark with \emph{zero} identifier overlap with training, and
we verified this on the released files rather than merely asserting it.
Because open-web retrieval cannot surface some conflict regimes, a curated
track supplies them, and we report its composition per class
(\S\ref{sec:data}).

Trace supervision's clearest gain is the refusal decision: F1 rises from as
low as $0.429$ without it to at least $0.826$ with it, and this gain holds
regardless of how we phrase the prompt, unlike baseline models, whose
behavior swings widely. The improvement lies in judgment, not answer
generation, since single-truth recall itself does not improve.

\textbf{Contributions.} \textbf{(1) A conflict-aware RAG resource}
(\S\ref{sec:data}): 943 training/validation examples and a disjoint 736-example
benchmark. Each of the $10{,}343$ adjudicated evidence units carries a verdict,
key fact, snippet-anchored quote, rationale, source-quality label, and the full
annotator vote tally. \textbf{(2) Evidence that trace supervision buys judgment,
not answer quality} (\S\ref{sec:results}): refusal calibration and
prompt-invariance improve, but single-truth recall and grounding under
well-specified prompts do not. \textbf{(3) CATS} (\S\ref{sec:cats}): an
evaluation framework that scores a conflict-aware response as four separate
components rather than one number, each reported over its own applicability
denominator. We validate three of the four components against an independent
human study and compute the fourth, grounded refusal, deterministically.
\textbf{(4) Oracle
ablations} (\S\ref{sec:oracle}): supplying gold information one stage at a time
localises which stage limits the system and gives construct-validity evidence for
the three-stage decomposition.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Knowledge and Evidence Conflicts}

We scope this paper to conflicts \emph{among retrieved documents}. A model's
parametric knowledge is the information it memorized during training, as
opposed to what it retrieves at inference time. Conflict between that
knowledge and retrieved evidence, the context--memory setting targeted by much
recent work \citep{huo2025microact,zhang2025faithfulrag,ye2026seeingthrough},
is out of scope here. We evaluate our systems only on whether they reason
correctly over the supplied set. Retrieval-augmented generation conditions a model on corpus-retrieved documents at
generation time
\citep{guu2020realm,lewis2020rag,izacard2023atlas,borgeaud2022,gao2024retrievalaugmentedgenerationlargelanguage,ram-etal-2023-context}.
Retrieved documents routinely carry conflicting information, which complicates
generation and has become a research area of its own
\citep{xu2024knowledgeconflictsllmssurvey}. \textit{ConflictBank} \citep{su2024conflictbankbenchmarkevaluatinginfluence} evaluates how models handle conflicts within retrieved knowledge, within parametric knowledge, and in their interplay. It pairs Wikidata claims with LLM-generated contradictory evidence across multiple textual styles. \textit{WikiContradict} \citep{hou2024wikicontradict} benchmarks robustness to explicit and implicit contradictions between Wikipedia passages, with human-annotated instances. \textit{ECON} \citep{jiayang2024econ} studies detection and resolution of evidence conflicts directly.
\textit{DRAGged into Conflicts} \citep{cattan2025dragged} is closest to our
setting. It proposes a conflict taxonomy spanning temporal, debatable,
misinformation, and complementary cases. It demonstrates that models often fail
to adapt their responses without explicit conflict-aware guidance. Most of this
literature focuses on \emph{detecting} or \emph{classifying} conflict.
Our framework instead supervises the reasoning that produces the
classification. It evaluates whether a model's document-level judgments,
conflict inference, and final response behaviour are mutually consistent,
rather than scoring only the final answer.

\subsection{Reasoning Supervision, Multi-Hop and Robustness in RAG}

Several lines of work supervise or elicit intermediate reasoning.
\textit{Chain-of-Thought} prompting established that generating intermediate
steps improves complex reasoning \citep{wei2022cot}. \textit{Chain-of-Note}
\citep{yu2024chainofnote} adapts this to retrieval by generating sequential
reading notes for each retrieved document. This improves robustness to noisy
and irrelevant passages and lets a model answer ``unknown'' when neither
retrieved nor intrinsic knowledge suffices. Adaptive adversarial training
\citep{fang2024enhancingnoiserobustnessretrievalaugmented} and robustness
training \citep{yoran2024makingretrievalaugmentedlanguagemodels} mitigate
retrieval noise, and \textit{GSM-IC} \citep{shi2023largelanguagemodelseasily}
shows that irrelevant context substantially degrades reasoning. Our work differs in two respects. First, each stage has a closed label set, so
Stage-1 and Stage-2 decisions are separately scorable against gold annotations
rather than merely inspectable. Second and more importantly, \textit{we treat
the reasoning trace as an object of evaluation rather than a means of improving
the answer.} The three-stage decomposition earns its place only if the stages
are genuinely separable. We test this directly by supplying gold information
for one stage at a time. We then ask whether each intervention moves only the
metrics that stage should govern (\S\ref{sec:oracle}).

\subsection{Measuring Trust, Groundedness and Conflict Alignment}

Trustworthiness metrics for RAG largely score an answer against the retrieved
set as a whole. Conflict-aware evaluation instead requires that the
\emph{expected} response depend on the kind of disagreement present. A single
scoring rule should not treat a balanced summary and a decisive correction the
same way. Evaluations of RAG frequently assess end-to-end system quality
\citep{gao2024retrievalaugmentedgenerationlargelanguage,xu2024aliiceevaluatingpositionalfinegrained},
conflating retriever quality with generator capability
\citep{fan2024surveyragmeetingllms}. \textit{Trust-Score} \citep{song2025trustalign}
advances this by evaluating trustworthiness through answer
correctness, citation reliability, and refusal behaviour. Its companion
\textit{Trust-Align} aligns models to improve that score. Trust-Align's
held-out refusal material forms the refusal track of our resource.
\textit{FaithEval}
\citep{ming2025faitheval} probes whether models stay faithful to context under
adversarial or counterfactual conditions. CATS extends this line in three ways. It conditions the expected response on
the inferred conflict type. It evaluates grounding at the claim level with
citation sensitivity. It also reports every component over its own
applicability denominator, since the four constructs apply to different
subsets of examples. Conflating them would confound the comparison the
framework aims to support (\S\ref{sec:whatdoesnot}). We
validate the judges against an independent human study \citep{zheng2023judging}
and report which components that validation supports and which it does not
(\S\ref{sec:human}).
